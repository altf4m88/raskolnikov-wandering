    Landmarks on the map

      About this map

      Raskolnikov's walks in Crime and Punishment, scene by scene, over today's street plan of St Petersburg. Every quotation is checked word for word against Constance Garnett's 1914 translation. Every placename is checked against the Russian.

      How to read it

      textual the novel names the place outright.
      traditional the novel hides it behind an initial ("S—— Lane", "K—— Bridge") and local tradition has filled in the address.
      conjectural no address exists in the text or the tradition; the pin is my guess, and the card says what it rests on.

      Solid lines are walks whose streets the text gives or forces. Dashed lines are routes I inferred as the shortest plausible walk between two scenes; the book usually says nothing about them. The fever band under the timeline is an editorial reading of his state of mind, not data.

      Chronology

      • The action covers about fourteen days. The novel has no day numbers, so I count from its own cues. Razumikhin says he has been ill "for the last four days". Part VI begins with "two or three days" of fog. Katerina Ivanovna's funeral, Porfiry's last visit and Svidrigailov's last night all fall on one day. Other reconstructions come out a day shorter or longer.
      • "Early in July" is Old Style. Russia used the Julian calendar, so by ours it is the second half of July. The sky in each scene is computed for that date at 59.93° N, in Petersburg local mean time, which is what clocks showed in 1865.
      • That makes Dostoevsky's light literary rather than exact. "Nearly eight o'clock, the sun was setting" (Part II) is more than an hour early; the sun set around 20:55 that week.

      Where the map is uncertain

      • The base map is today's OpenStreetMap: present-day building footprints, capped at six storeys to suggest the 1860s skyline. Bridges built after 1865 (Palace, Trinity, Birzhevoy, Betancourt) are removed and the routing avoids them.
      • The garret (Grazhdanskaya 19 / Stolyarny 5) and the pawnbroker's flat (Srednyaya Podyacheskaya 15 / Canal 104) are traditional but still argued over. The police office has two traditions: Canal 67, used here, and Sadovaya 28.
      • Kozel's house, the tavern, Porfiry's "grey house", Potchinkov's, Bakaleyev's house number and Svidrigailov's watch-tower have no agreed address.
      • The 730 paces. At an ordinary 0.71 m pace, 730 paces is about 520 m. The text sends him "towards K. bridge", and that route measures about 1.1 km. Even the shortest street route today is about 0.7 km. Either Raskolnikov took enormous strides, or the number is a novelist's exactness, not a surveyor's.

      Checked against a printed edition

      The Wordsworth Classics edition (Garnett translation, introduction by Keith Carabine) prints a hand-drawn map, "The St Petersburg of Crime and Punishment", with eight numbered sites. Each place it marks says on its scene card whether that edition agrees.

      • Agrees: Raskolnikov's room (1), the pawnbroker's (2), St Isaac's (4), the Nikolaevsky Bridge (6), Konnogvardeisky Boulevard, and Svidrigailov's death on Bolshoy Prospekt, Petersburg Side (8). Both maps guess the exact spot on that street; the book's is nearer its western end.
      • Differs: Sonia's room (3). The book draws it east of Kokushkin Bridge, toward the Hay Market. This map keeps the tradition of Canal 73, west of the bridge, because Part V puts her house two doors from a bridge whose hidden name ends in "-sky". Voznesensky fits; Sennoy and Demidov, to the east, do not.
      • Corrected from the book: the "X. Prospect" off the Hay Market. The book's "Obukhovsky Prospect" is the 1865 name of today's Moskovsky Prospekt; this map had used "Zabalkansky", a name it only got in 1878.
      • Added from the book: the University (7), on Vasilievsky Island, where Raskolnikov studied until his money ran out.
      • Where the book is wrong: its introduction says Petrovsky Island "contains the fortress of St Peter and St Paul", and its map writes "Petrovsky Island" across the Petersburg Side. The fortress stands on Zayachy (Hare) Island. Petrovsky Island is the separate island west of the Zhdanovka, where Raskolnikov dreams of the mare. Its Winter Palace (5) is also drawn away from the Neva, and Gorokhovaya is misspelt "Gorakhavya".
      • The book's map is a sketch, not to scale, so it can confirm which street or bank a place is on, not which house.

      Garnett against the Russian

      • "S. Place": the Russian is С—м переулке, S—— Lane.
      • "walking along Vassilyevsky Prospect": the Russian is через В—й проспект, the V—— Prospect (Voznesensky), the road to the island.
      • "near the bridge to Vassilyevsky Ostrov": the Russian puts Razumikhin on the island, on the Little Neva embankment by the bridge.
      • "That's in Voskresensky": the Russian is на Вознесенском, Voznesensky Prospekt.
      • "Third Street in Vassilyevsky Island": the Russian is в Третьей линии, на Малом проспекте, the Third Line at Maly Prospekt.
      • Katerina Ivanovna's last cry, «Уездили клячу!» ("they've driven the nag to death"), becomes "I am done for", and the echo of the mare dream is lost.
      • Clock times: Russian counts the hour in progress. «во втором часу» is between one and two, and «часу в седьмом» between six and seven; Garnett rounds them up to "two o'clock" and "about seven". The scenes here use the Russian.
      • Svidrigailov's sentry speaks in a caricatured Jewish accent in the Russian («А-зе здеся нельзя, здеся не места!»), part of a stereotyped portrait; Garnett puts the line into plain English.

      The walker

      A former student, a garret, an idea

      Rodion Raskolnikov is twenty-three, penniless, and lodges in a room off the Hay Market that is “more like a cupboard than a room”. For a month he has been rehearsing a crime in his head.

      Over fourteen days he walks these streets: to a pawnbroker’s flat, down into taverns, across the bridges and out to the islands, to the police office, and to Sonia.
